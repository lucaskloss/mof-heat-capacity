# Metatrain LLPR uncertainty for MOF-5 trajectories

This guide explains last-layer prediction rigidity (LLPR), how the current
`metatrain` implementation exposes it, and how it could be connected to this
project's trajectory and heat-capacity analysis. It was checked against the
official documentation and source on 2026-08-26:

- [Computing LLPR uncertainties](https://docs.metatensor.org/metatrain/latest/generated_examples/1-advanced/01-llpr.html)
- [LLPR architecture reference](https://docs.metatensor.org/metatrain/latest/architectures/generated/llpr.html)
- [LLPR-derived shallow ensembles](https://docs.metatensor.org/metatrain/latest/generated_examples/1-advanced/08-llpr-ensemble-training.html)
- [Metatrain auxiliary outputs](https://docs.metatensor.org/metatrain/latest/concepts/auxiliary-outputs.html)
- [Metatomic ASE integration](https://docs.metatensor.org/metatomic/latest/engines/ase.html)
- [Metatomic LAMMPS integration](https://docs.metatensor.org/metatomic/latest/engines/lammps.html)
- G. Bigi *et al.*, [*A prediction rigidity formalism for low-cost
  uncertainties in trained neural networks*](https://doi.org/10.1088/2632-2153/ad805f)

The software interfaces described by `latest` can change. Record the actual
`metatrain`, `metatomic`, `metatomic-ase`, Torch, and exported-model versions
with every result.

## Decision in one paragraph

Use analytical LLPR first as a cheap, calibrated **domain-of-applicability
diagnostic** for saved MOF-5 trajectories. Request both the total-system
`energy_uncertainty` and local per-atom values to locate uncertain methane
contacts, framework distortions, or adsorption environments. To propagate
MLIP uncertainty into ensemble averages and heat capacity as described by
Imbalzano *et al.*, analytical LLPR alone is insufficient: generate and save
one LLPR shallow ensemble and request its member-resolved `energy_ensemble`
output. Evaluate that same ensemble on all temperatures and replicas, reweight
the member enthalpy curves, and differentiate each member curve before taking
their spread. Keep this MLIP contribution separate from autocorrelation,
replica, Hessian, and between-MLIP uncertainties.

## 1. What LLPR measures

Consider a trained neural network whose last layer is linear in a feature vector
$\mathbf f$ produced by all preceding layers. Let $\mathbf F$ collect the
last-layer features of the training structures. Metatrain uses the regularized
LLPR variance

$$\sigma_*^2 =\alpha^2\mathbf f_*^\mathrm{T} \left( \mathbf F^\mathrm{T}\mathbf F+\varsigma^2\mathbf I \right)^{-1} \mathbf f_*. $$

Here:

- $\mathbf f_*$ is the last-layer feature vector of a new structure;
- $\mathbf F^\mathrm{T}\mathbf F$ records which feature-space directions are
  constrained by the training data;
- $\varsigma$ regularizes poorly conditioned directions; and
- $\alpha$ is a global scale fitted to validation residuals.

The prediction rigidity is the reciprocal of the variance. A well-constrained
feature direction has high rigidity and low uncertainty. A novel or weakly
constrained direction has low rigidity and high uncertainty.

Metatrain returns $\sigma_*$, the **standard deviation**, not $\sigma_*^2$ and
not the rigidity itself. The result has the same unit as its target: for an
energy model exported in eV, `energy_uncertainty` is in eV.

### The last-layer approximation

LLPR treats the representation generated by the preceding neural-network
layers as fixed and attributes uncertainty to the final linear readout. This is
why it is inexpensive: constructing LLPR needs one pass over the training data
to accumulate $\mathbf F^\mathrm{T}\mathbf F$, rather than retraining many deep
models. Inference reuses the ordinary model features and a precomputed Cholesky
factor.

This approximation does **not** quantify all epistemic uncertainty. Shared
errors in the learned representation, architecture, cutoff, long-range physics,
or reference electronic structure can remain invisible. Low LLPR uncertainty
means that the final readout is well constrained in its feature space; it does
not prove that the potential-energy surface is correct.

## 2. Construction and calibration

LLPR wraps an already trained model. The official workflow is:

1. load the original model **checkpoint**, not just its exported `.pt` file;
2. run the original training structures through the model to construct the
   last-layer feature covariance;
3. regularize and factorize that covariance;
4. calibrate a multiplier $\alpha$ from reference residuals on a validation
   set; and
5. export a new model containing the original prediction plus LLPR outputs.

As of the documentation date above, the metatrain wrapper officially supports
neural-network models built with the PET and SOAP-BPNN architectures.

The tutorial describes this as roughly one additional training epoch without
backpropagation. It is cheap relative to training the base PET model, but it
still requires access to its structures, target definitions, units, and
reference labels.

### Calibration choices

The current LLPR trainer supports three Gaussian calibration methods:

| `calibration_method` | Scale fitted by metatrain | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `absolute_residuals` | Mean $|r|/\sigma_\mathrm{raw}$ with the Gaussian MAE-to-$\sigma$ factor | Current default; more robust to large outliers and recommended by the docs for large or uncurated datasets. |
| `squared_residuals` | Root mean $r^2/\sigma_\mathrm{raw}^2$ | Equivalent to optimizing a Gaussian negative log likelihood with respect to the global scale. |
| `crps` | Scale minimizing Gaussian continuous ranked probability score | Useful when a later shallow ensemble is trained with a CRPS loss. |

Calibration supplies one global multiplier per uncertainty output or channel.
It cannot repair a feature representation that assigns the wrong *relative*
uncertainties to different configurations.

For this project, the validation set should contain decorrelated structures
covering each reported temperature, methane loading, adsorption regime, and
relevant framework distortion. Their reference energies must use the same
electronic-structure definition as the model's labels. Do not calibrate and
judge coverage on the same small collection of frames.

### Regularization

If `regularizer` is left as `null`, metatrain increases a small diagonal term
until the covariance admits a stable Cholesky decomposition. A user-specified
value overrides that search. The selected regularizer affects uncertainty in
weakly sampled feature directions; record it and test sensitivity rather than
treating it as an irrelevant implementation detail.

### Minimal configuration pattern

The following is an illustrative pattern, not a ready-to-run project file. The
training and validation paths and target keys must match the original PET
training setup and reference units.

```yaml
device: cuda
base_precision: 64
seed: 2025

architecture:
  name: llpr
  training:
    model_checkpoint: models/base-pet-model.ckpt
    batch_size: 4
    regularizer: null
    calibration_method: absolute_residuals

training_set:
  systems: path/to/original-training-structures.xyz
  targets:
    energy:
      key: energy
      unit: eV

validation_set:
  systems: path/to/reference-validation-structures.xyz
  targets:
    energy:
      key: energy
      unit: eV

test_set: 0.0
```

Metatrain then follows the same command pattern as the tutorial:

```bash
mtt train options-llpr.yaml -o model-llpr.pt
```

The official installation command is `pip install metatrain[llpr]`. This
project deliberately pins a CUDA-12-compatible Torch build for Izar. Do not let
an LLPR installation replace that pin: first resolve and test a compatible
`metatrain` version in a cloned environment, then record it in the production
environment specification.

## 3. Analytical LLPR and local LPR outputs

An LLPR-wrapped energy model adds `energy_uncertainty` while preserving the
ordinary `energy` output. With `sample_kind="system"`, it returns one calibrated
standard deviation per structure. With `sample_kind="atom"`, it returns local
prediction-rigidity uncertainties, usually called LPR values.

The official ASE pattern is:

```python
from metatomic.torch import ModelOutput
from metatomic_ase import MetatomicCalculator

calculator = MetatomicCalculator("model-llpr.pt", device="cuda")

system_outputs = calculator.run_model(
    structures,
    {
        "energy": ModelOutput(sample_kind="system"),
        "energy_uncertainty": ModelOutput(sample_kind="system"),
    },
)

local_outputs = calculator.run_model(
    structure,
    {
        "energy_uncertainty": ModelOutput(sample_kind="atom"),
    },
)
```

The returned objects are metatensor `TensorMap`s. For a simple scalar target,
the arrays are obtained from
`outputs[name].block().values.squeeze().cpu().numpy()`.

### How to use local uncertainty

For a loaded MOF trajectory, local values can answer questions such as:

- Are the largest uncertainties on methane, the Zn--O nodes, linkers, or
  unusually close host--guest contacts?
- Does local uncertainty increase when methane hops between adsorption sites?
- Are high-temperature framework distortions outside the training domain?
- Which decorrelated frames should be selected for new reference calculations?

Store the maximum, high percentiles, and host/methane-group summaries for every
analyzed frame. Preserve full per-atom values for selected high-uncertainty
frames so they can be visualized.

Local energy contributions are not unique physical observables. Do not sum
local standard deviations to obtain the total-system uncertainty, and do not
interpret an atom's LPR value as a literal error assigned only to that atom.
Use the separately requested system-level value for total-energy uncertainty.

## 4. The shallow LLPR ensemble

Analytical LLPR provides a mean and standard deviation but no signed,
member-resolved potential differences. Metatrain can also sample final-layer
weights from the LLPR Gaussian approximation. Add, for example,

```yaml
architecture:
  name: llpr
  model:
    num_ensemble_members:
      energy: 32
  training:
    model_checkpoint: models/base-pet-model.ckpt
    batch_size: 4
```

The value `32` comes from the official tutorial; it is not a universal
convergence choice. Increase it until the uncertainty of the final observable
is stable.

The exported model then provides:

| Output | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `energy` | Original central model prediction. |
| `energy_uncertainty` | Analytical calibrated LLPR standard deviation. |
| `energy_ensemble` | One energy prediction for each sampled last-layer member. |

The current implementation recenters the finite ensemble so that its arithmetic
mean equals the original model prediction on every configuration. This matters
for existing trajectories: if they were generated by the exact same base PET
checkpoint, the original potential is also the LLPR ensemble-mean potential.
They can therefore be useful mean-potential trajectories for post-processing,
without rerunning MD solely to change the force driver.

Requesting all three outputs follows the official example:

```python
outputs = calculator.run_model(
    structures,
    {
        "energy": ModelOutput(sample_kind="system"),
        "energy_uncertainty": ModelOutput(sample_kind="system"),
        "energy_ensemble": ModelOutput(sample_kind="system"),
    },
)

energies = outputs["energy"].block().values.squeeze().cpu().numpy()
sigmas = outputs["energy_uncertainty"].block().values.squeeze().cpu().numpy()
member_energies = outputs["energy_ensemble"].block().values.squeeze().cpu().numpy()
```

For a batch of frames, `member_energies` is expected to have one row per frame
and one column per ensemble member. Verify the shape and the equality of
`member_energies.mean(axis=1)` and `energies` before analysis.

### Optional ensemble training

Setting `num_epochs` in the LLPR training section enables gradient-based tuning
of the shallow ensemble weights, using an ensemble probabilistic loss such as
Gaussian NLL or CRPS. This can improve calibration but costs additional
training. If `train_all_parameters: true` is used, the base representation also
changes; the official documentation warns that only the ensemble spread, not
the original analytical LLPR output, remains meaningful in that case.

The documented analytical LLPR construction does not yet use gradient labels
such as conservative forces and stresses. Current LLPR ensemble code can expose
position and strain derivatives of energy-member predictions, but that is not
the same as a separately calibrated force- or stress-uncertainty model. Validate
member forces and stresses before using an LLPR ensemble to drive NPT dynamics.

## 5. Relationship to the Imbalzano committee method

The two approaches share a goal but define their model ensembles differently:

| Imbalzano calibrated committee | Metatrain LLPR shallow ensemble |
| --- | --- |
| Retrains multiple full models on resampled datasets. | Shares one nonlinear representation and samples only final-layer weights. |
| Committee spread is calibrated to validation errors. | Analytical covariance is calibrated by $\alpha$ before weights are sampled. |
| Captures variability from data resampling throughout the model. | Uses a last-layer/Laplace approximation and misses shared representation uncertainty. |
| Provides member energies for reweighting. | `energy_ensemble` provides member energies for the same reweighting machinery. |

The LLPR shallow ensemble is therefore a computationally attractive
approximation to the potential-model committee required for reweighting. It is
not equivalent to a deep ensemble, and its thermodynamic error bars must be
validated against reference frames and, if affordable, a smaller independently
trained committee.

Analytical `energy_uncertainty` alone cannot evaluate
$\mathrm{Cov}(a,V^{(i)}-\bar V)$ because it discards the signs and correlations
of model deviations between frames. Do not generate independent Gaussian
energy noise on each frame: that destroys precisely the cross-configuration
correlations that control Boltzmann reweighting.

## 6. Integration with existing trajectory analysis

The safest first implementation is a separate, read-only property stage. Keep
LAMMPS production unchanged, evaluate LLPR on saved frames with
`MetatomicCalculator.run_model`, and write a sidecar archive. This respects the
repository's separation between MD and property calculation and avoids paying
for uncertainty on every integration step.

### Required per-frame data

For every production frame, store:

- configuration identity, timestep, temperature, replica, loading, and central
  model identity;
- cell and volume;
- central potential energy $\bar V_t$;
- system analytical uncertainty $\sigma_{V,t}$;
- local uncertainty summaries and selected full per-atom arrays;
- member energies $V_t^{(i)}$ from one persistent LLPR ensemble; and
- the matching kinetic energy from the LAMMPS thermo log if the analysis keeps
  the sampled kinetic contribution explicitly.

Process frames in batches on the GPU and store arrays incrementally. A full
$N_\mathrm{frames}\times N_\mathrm{atoms}$ local matrix and an
$N_\mathrm{frames}\times M$ member matrix can be large, so choose the analysis
stride from autocorrelation and event-resolution requirements rather than the
MD timestep.

### Model identity across temperatures

Generate the LLPR ensemble once and reuse the exact exported file everywhere.
The same member index must identify the same sampled last-layer weights at every
temperature and in every replica. Regenerating a 32-member ensemble separately
for each job destroys the cross-temperature covariance needed when
differentiating enthalpy.

For PET-MAD and PET-SOL, construct and analyze two separate LLPR models. Their
central-curve difference remains between-MLIP sensitivity; it should not be
mixed into either model's calibrated LLPR variance.

### Reweighting enthalpy

For member $i$ on frame $t$, define

$$\Delta V_t^{(i)}=V_t^{(i)}-\bar V_t, $$

$$H_t^{(i)}=K_t+V_t^{(i)}+P_\mathrm{ext}V_{\mathrm{cell},t}. $$

At fixed $T$, exact reweighting gives

$$\left\langle H^{(i)}\right\rangle_{V^{(i)}} =\frac{ \left\langle e^{-\beta\Delta V^{(i)}}H^{(i)}\right\rangle_{\bar V} }{ \left\langle e^{-\beta\Delta V^{(i)}}\right\rangle_{\bar V} }. $$

For the large MOF system, first-order cumulant expansion is likely more stable,

$$\left\langle H^{(i)}\right\rangle_{V^{(i)}} \approx \left\langle H^{(i)}\right\rangle_{\bar V} -\beta\,\mathrm{Cov}_{\bar V} \left(H^{(i)},\Delta V^{(i)}\right). $$

Estimate means and covariances with correlation-aware blocks. Constant
member-energy offsets cancel in normalized exponential weights and in the CEA
covariance; center $\Delta V^{(i)}$ before exponentiating for numerical
stability.

At each temperature, retain one reweighted mean enthalpy for every member. Apply
the same temperature derivative to each complete member curve,

$$C_{P,\mathrm{cl}}^{(i)}(T) =\frac{d\left\langle H^{(i)}\right\rangle_{V^{(i)}}}{dT}, $$

and take the member spread afterward. Do not independently draw an LLPR error
at each temperature before differentiating; that loses member correlations and
can seriously distort the heat-capacity band.

### Exact-weight overlap

Compute the normalized weights and an effective sample count such as

$$N_\mathrm{eff}^{(i)} =\frac{\left(\sum_t w_t^{(i)}\right)^2} {\sum_t\left(w_t^{(i)}\right)^2}. $$

This diagnostic is not a guarantee, but a very small $N_\mathrm{eff}$ clearly
shows that exact reweighting is dominated by a few frames. Compare exact and
CEA results and run selected member-driven trajectories at representative
temperatures before accepting the approximation.

### If only analytical LLPR is available

The scalar output is still useful for trajectory screening, active-learning
selection, and the conservative pointwise-only upper bound discussed by
Imbalzano *et al.* It does not support exact or CEA member reweighting. Label any
band derived only from scalar LLPR as a diagnostic bound, not as the preferred
propagated model uncertainty.

## 7. LAMMPS options

The metatomic LAMMPS pair style recognizes `energy_uncertainty`. Its
`uncertainty_threshold` option warns when the maximum local energy uncertainty
exceeds a threshold; the documented default is $100$ meV/atom. This is a
warning mechanism, not an abort, fallback potential, or active-learning loop.
Choose a threshold from calibrated validation behavior rather than accepting
the default as a scientific criterion.

The separate `compute metatomic` command can evaluate arbitrary exported-model
outputs and place scalars or vectors in logs/dumps. In principle it can request
`energy_uncertainty` or an `energy_ensemble` vector. Doing so may load and run an
additional model evaluation and enlarge trajectory output substantially.
Offline ASE post-processing is preferable until timing and output semantics
have been validated for the project's pinned LAMMPS build.

## 8. Compatibility with the current repository

Local inspection shows that the current exported PET-MAD and PET-SOL models
contain `mtt::aux::energy_last_layer_features`, and their checkpoints contain
PET architecture metadata and last-layer parameter names. These are the
interfaces required by the LLPR wrapper, so compatibility looks plausible.

It is not yet a production-ready path:

- `metatrain[llpr]` is not declared or pinned in `environment.yml`;
- the original training structures and reference targets required to construct
  $\mathbf F^\mathrm{T}\mathbf F$ are not present in this repository;
- a representative, reference-labeled MOF/methane validation set is also
  needed for calibration and coverage testing;
- checkpoint loading and export must be tested against the project's pinned
  Torch/CUDA stack; and
- LAMMPS NPT stress behavior must be revalidated if an LLPR-wrapped model is
  ever used as the force driver.

The existing central-model trajectories remain potentially useful. Because the
generated LLPR ensemble is recentered on the original PET prediction, they can
be post-processed once a compatible LLPR ensemble has been constructed. Verify
central energies frame by frame before relying on this equivalence.

### Harmonic correction limitation

LLPR operates on the metatrain/metatomic model. The current harmonic workflow
uses a separate PET-JAX representation. Metatrain does not document a path that
exports every sampled LLPR last-layer member to PET-JAX. Therefore the first
LLPR integration should quantify uncertainty in the **classical NPT term only**.
Continue to report minimum-to-minimum and numerical uncertainty for the harmonic
correction separately.

Do not label the complete hybrid curve's error as fully propagated MLIP
uncertainty until member-specific Hessians can be computed consistently or the
neglected harmonic model contribution has been bounded.

## 9. Validation checklist

Before using LLPR values as error bars:

- verify that the LLPR model reproduces the base model's central energies,
  forces, and stresses to numerical precision;
- keep training, calibration, and final test structures disjoint;
- plot normalized validation residuals
  $z=(E_\mathrm{model}-E_\mathrm{ref})/\sigma_E$ and check their mean, spread,
  tails, and empirical one-, two-, and three-$\sigma$ coverage;
- bin frames by predicted variance and compare the mean squared reference error
  with predicted variance, as in the LLPR paper;
- test calibration separately by temperature, loading, methane environment,
  and framework-distortion regime;
- assess relative-energy errors within a fixed composition, because constant
  total-energy offsets cancel from Boltzmann reweighting even though they can
  dominate absolute residuals;
- converge the regularizer, number of ensemble members, frame stride, and
  correlation-aware covariance estimates;
- verify that the finite ensemble mean equals the central prediction on every
  analyzed frame;
- compare analytical LLPR standard deviations with the sampled ensemble spread;
- compare exact reweighting, CEA, and selected direct member trajectories; and
- keep LLPR, finite-trajectory, replica, Hessian, between-MLIP, and reference-
  method uncertainties as named components.

## Required provenance

Record at least:

- base checkpoint and exported-model hashes;
- `metatrain`, `metatomic`, `metatomic-ase`, Torch, CUDA, and LAMMPS versions;
- original covariance dataset and target/unit definition;
- calibration and test datasets, reference method, and split policy;
- calibration method, fitted $\alpha$, and regularizer;
- shallow-ensemble seed, saved model hash, member count, and any further
  ensemble training;
- trajectory/model identity check, analysis stride, and frame selection;
- local/system output convention and units;
- exact-weight effective sample counts and CEA comparison; and
- how LLPR uncertainty was combined with, or kept separate from, other error
  sources.

## Bottom line

LLPR offers a practical way to add uncertainty information to the existing PET
models without training a full deep committee. Its analytical output is best
used to diagnose whether MOF/methane environments are supported by the training
features. Its shallow ensemble is the form needed for thermodynamic reweighting
and heat-capacity propagation. The current PET exports appear structurally
compatible, and existing trajectories may be reusable, but calibrated results
still require the original training data, relevant reference validation data,
a pinned LLPR installation, and explicit validation of reweighting and NPT
behavior.
