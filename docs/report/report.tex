\begin{abstract}
Methane-loaded MOF-5 heat capacity is estimated by combining classical NPT
molecular dynamics with a loaded-system harmonic quantum correction from
PET-JAX/SADMOF Hessians. PET-MAD and PET-SOL calculations at 50, 100, and
150 methane molecules give exploratory room-temperature values of about
0.93, 1.07--1.08, and \SI{1.16}{J\per g\per K}. Sampling errors and paired
last-layer prediction-rigidity (LLPR) model uncertainties are propagated
separately. Production drift, imaginary modes, incomplete relaxation, and
sensitivity to uncertainty propagation prevent a claim of convergence.
Variation across quenched minima remains unestimated. The empty-framework
Hessian provides a separate reference.
\end{abstract}

\section{Scientific motivation and scope}

MOF-5 combines high-frequency bond vibrations with soft framework motion;
methane adds internal vibrations and anharmonic motion within the pores.
Classical MD samples the latter, while normal modes correct the excessive
classical occupation of high-frequency vibrations. The conventional host
cell contains 424 atoms,
$\mathrm{C}_{192}\mathrm{H}_{96}\mathrm{O}_{104}\mathrm{Zn}_{32}$;
50, 100, and 150 methane molecules increase the totals to 674, 924, and 1174.

Figure~\ref{fig:workflow} summarizes the calculation. All loaded terms use
the same PET checkpoint, composition, and mass normalization. The aim is to
test the hybrid approximation of Kapil \emph{et al.}\ \cite{kapil2019}
on PET through convergence and validation, rather than matching their
force-field results.

\section{Theory}
\label{sec:theory}

\subsection{Statistical mechanics for molecular dynamics}
\label{sec:statistical-mechanics}

The required observable is the fixed-loading, constant-pressure heat
capacity. Equilibrium distributions define its averages; correlations
limit the information in finite trajectories \cite{allen2017,tuckerman2023}.

\subsubsection{Microscopic model and equations of motion}

The Born--Oppenheimer potential $U(\bm R;\bm h)$ describes electronic energy
as a function of nuclear positions. Treating nuclei classically is a separate
approximation. With momenta $\bm p_i=m_i\bm v_i$ and volume $V=\det\bm h$,
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal H(\bm R,\bm p;\bm h)
  =K(\bm p)+U(\bm R;\bm h),\qquad
  K=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\bm p_i^2}{2m_i}.
  \label{eq:classical-hamiltonian}
\end{equation}
Physical internal energy is $E=K+U$, and enthalpy is
$H=E+P_{\mathrm{ext}}V$. Hamilton's equations give the forces:
\begin{equation}
  \dot{\bm R}_i=\frac{\partial\mathcal H}{\partial\bm p_i}
  =\frac{\bm p_i}{m_i},\qquad
  \dot{\bm p}_i=-\frac{\partial\mathcal H}{\partial\bm R_i}
  =\bm F_i.
  \label{eq:hamilton-equations}
\end{equation}
Exact fixed-cell dynamics conserves $E$ for a time-independent potential.
Reservoir couplings exchange heat and volume. Classical nuclei omit
zero-point motion, tunneling, and quantum populations; a vibrational mode
is approximately classical when $k_{\mathrm B}T\gg\hbar\omega$.

\subsubsection{Phase space and equilibrium averages}

A microstate is $\Gamma=(\bm R,\bm p)$. For unconstrained Cartesian nuclei,
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm d\Gamma
  =\frac{\mathrm d^{3N}R\,\mathrm d^{3N}p}
  {h_{\mathrm P}^{3N}\prod_s N_s!},
\end{equation}
Here $h_{\mathrm P}$ is Planck's constant and $N_s$ counts identical particles
of species $s$. These normalization factors cancel in fixed-composition
averages; constraints require a reduced measure.
\begin{equation}
  \langle A\rangle=\int\mathrm d\Gamma\,\rho(\Gamma)A(\Gamma),
  \qquad \int\mathrm d\Gamma\,\rho(\Gamma)=1.
  \label{eq:ensemble-average}
\end{equation}
Hamiltonian flow preserves phase-space volume:
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\partial\rho}{\partial t}
  +\sum_i\left(
    \dot{\bm R}_i\cdot\nabla_{\bm R_i}\rho
    +\dot{\bm p}_i\cdot\nabla_{\bm p_i}\rho\right)=0.
\end{equation}
Equating an ensemble average with a long-time average additionally assumes
ergodicity:
\begin{equation}
  \langle A\rangle
  =\lim_{\tau\to\infty}\frac{1}{\tau}
   \int_0^\tau A\bigl(\Gamma(t)\bigr)\,\mathrm dt.
  \label{eq:ergodic-average}
\end{equation}
Equilibration, barrier crossing, and guest redistribution must be tested
with structural diagnostics and independent replicas; stationary temperature
alone does not establish adequate sampling.

\subsubsection{Equilibrium ensembles}

NVE fixes energy and volume; NVT exchanges heat; NPT also exchanges volume.
All retain fixed composition and therefore do not determine an adsorption
isotherm. Their equilibrium weights are
\begin{equation}
  \rho_{NVE}(\Gamma)
  =\frac{\delta\bigl(E_0-\mathcal H(\Gamma)\bigr)}{\Omega(N,V,E_0)},
  \label{eq:microcanonical-density}
\end{equation}
where $\Omega=\int\mathrm d\Gamma\,\delta(E_0-\mathcal H)$ and additional
invariants restrict the energy shell. NVE is useful for energy-conservation
tests. With $\beta=(k_{\mathrm B}T)^{-1}$, canonical sampling uses
\begin{align}
  Z(N,V,T)&=\int\mathrm d\Gamma\,e^{-\beta\mathcal H(\Gamma)},\\
  \rho_{NVT}(\Gamma)&=Z^{-1}e^{-\beta\mathcal H(\Gamma)}.
  \label{eq:canonical-density}
\end{align}
Integrating momenta gives a configurational weight $e^{-\beta U}$.
Partition functions connect these distributions to thermodynamics:
\begin{align}
  F&=-k_{\mathrm B}T\ln Z, &
  \langle E\rangle&=-\left(\frac{\partial\ln Z}{\partial\beta}\right)_{N,V},\\
  S&=-\left(\frac{\partial F}{\partial T}\right)_{N,V}, &
  P&=-\left(\frac{\partial F}{\partial V}\right)_{N,T}.
\end{align}
For variable volume at fixed cell shape,
\begin{equation}
  \Delta(N,P_{\mathrm{ext}},T)
  =\int_0^\infty\frac{\mathrm dV}{V_0}\,
    e^{-\beta P_{\mathrm{ext}}V}Z(N,V,T),
\end{equation}
where $V_0$ is a fixed reference volume, and
\begin{equation}
  \rho_{NPT}(\Gamma,V)
  =\Delta^{-1}e^{-\beta[\mathcal H(\Gamma;V)+P_{\mathrm{ext}}V]}.
  \label{eq:npt-density}
\end{equation}
Thus $G=-k_{\mathrm B}T\ln\Delta$,
$\langle H\rangle=-\partial_\beta\ln\Delta$, and
$\langle V\rangle=(\partial G/\partial P_{\mathrm{ext}})_{N,T}$.
Use imposed pressure in $H$; exclude thermostat and barostat energies.

Fractional coordinates $\bm s_i=\bm h^{-1}\bm R_i$ give
$\mathrm d^{3N}R=V^N\mathrm d^{3N}s$. Flexible-cell NPT needs the correct
cell measure and extended-system corrections \cite{mtk}; the present
calculation permits anisotropic strain under hydrostatic external pressure.

\subsubsection{Equipartition and temperature}

Each independent quadratic term contributes $k_{\mathrm B}T/2$. For $f$
active momentum degrees of freedom,
\begin{equation}
  \langle K\rangle=\frac{f}{2}k_{\mathrm B}T,\qquad
  T_{\mathrm{kin}}=\frac{2K}{f k_{\mathrm B}}.
  \label{eq:kinetic-temperature}
\end{equation}
Subtract constraints and deliberately removed momentum components from
$3N$; fixing total linear momentum gives $f=3N-3$. Auxiliary reservoir
variables do not count. Initial velocities follow
\begin{equation}
  v_{i\alpha}\sim\mathcal N\!\left(0,\frac{k_{\mathrm B}T}{m_i}\right),
  \qquad \alpha\in\{x,y,z\}.
\end{equation}
Subtract center-of-mass velocity when required. Canonical fluctuations obey
$\operatorname{var}(K)=f(k_{\mathrm B}T)^2/2$ and
$\operatorname{var}(T_{\mathrm{kin}})=2T^2/f$; repeated exact rescaling
suppresses these fluctuations.

\subsubsection{Response functions and heat capacity}
\label{sec:md-response}

For a temperature-independent observable and Hamiltonian,
\begin{equation}
  \left(\frac{\partial\langle A\rangle}{\partial T}\right)_{N,V}
  =\frac{\operatorname{cov}(A,E)}{k_{\mathrm B}T^2},
\end{equation}
where $\operatorname{cov}(A,B)=\langle AB\rangle-\langle A\rangle\langle B\rangle$.
At fixed pressure, replace $E$ by physical enthalpy $H$:
\begin{align}
  C_V&=\left(\frac{\partial\langle E\rangle}{\partial T}\right)_{N,V}
      =\frac{\operatorname{var}_{NVT}(E)}{k_{\mathrm B}T^2},
      \label{eq:fluctuation-cv}\\
  C_P&=\left(\frac{\partial\langle H\rangle}{\partial T}\right)_{N,P_{\mathrm{ext}}}
      =\frac{\operatorname{var}_{NPT}(H)}{k_{\mathrm B}T^2}.
      \label{eq:fluctuation-cp}
\end{align}
For separable kinetic energy, equivalent configurational estimators are
\begin{align}
  C_V&=\frac{f}{2}k_{\mathrm B}
       +\frac{\operatorname{var}_{NVT}(U)}{k_{\mathrm B}T^2},\\
  C_P&=\frac{f}{2}k_{\mathrm B}
       +\frac{\operatorname{var}_{NPT}(U+P_{\mathrm{ext}}V)}{k_{\mathrm B}T^2}.
\end{align}
Omitting the kinetic term underestimates heat capacity. The NVT variance
formula cannot be applied to constant-energy NVE trajectories.
Volume responses similarly satisfy
\begin{align}
  \kappa_T&=-\frac{1}{\langle V\rangle}
    \left(\frac{\partial\langle V\rangle}{\partial P_{\mathrm{ext}}}\right)_{N,T}
    =\frac{\operatorname{var}_{NPT}(V)}{k_{\mathrm B}T\langle V\rangle},\\
  \alpha_V&=\frac{1}{\langle V\rangle}
    \left(\frac{\partial\langle V\rangle}{\partial T}\right)_{N,P_{\mathrm{ext}}}
    =\frac{\operatorname{cov}_{NPT}(V,H)}{k_{\mathrm B}T^2\langle V\rangle}.
\end{align}
For a stable hydrostatic phase,
\begin{equation}
  C_P-C_V=\frac{TV\alpha_V^2}{\kappa_T},
  \label{eq:cp-cv}
\end{equation}
Explicitly state-dependent effective potentials require additional
response terms. The present workflow instead differentiates mean NPT
enthalpies across temperatures (Eq.~\eqref{eq:finite-difference-cp}).
Smaller temperature spacing reduces discretization bias but amplifies noise.
Cell heat capacities become mass-specific values through
$c_P=C_P/M_{\mathrm{cell}}$, including guest mass.

\subsubsection{Correlation and sampling uncertainty}

Discard initial transients using running/block averages, structure, and
replica comparisons. Minimization does not replace thermal equilibration.
For $M$ production samples saved every $\delta t_{\mathrm s}$,
\begin{align}
  \overline A&=\frac{1}{M}\sum_{n=1}^{M}A_n,\\
  C_A(k)&=\langle(A_n-\langle A\rangle)
                 (A_{n+k}-\langle A\rangle)\rangle,\\
  \rho_A(k)&=C_A(k)/C_A(0).
\end{align}
The sample-mean variance is
\begin{equation}
  \operatorname{var}(\overline A)
  =\frac{C_A(0)}{M}\left[1+2\sum_{k=1}^{M-1}
       \left(1-\frac{k}{M}\right)\rho_A(k)\right].
\end{equation}
For runs much longer than the correlation time,
\begin{align}
  g_A&=1+2\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\rho_A(k),&
  \tau_{\mathrm{int},A}&=\frac{\delta t_{\mathrm s}}{2}g_A,\\
  M_{\mathrm{eff},A}&\simeq\frac{M}{g_A},&
  \operatorname{SE}(\overline A)&\simeq
       \sqrt{\frac{C_A(0)}{M_{\mathrm{eff},A}}}.
  \label{eq:md-standard-error}
\end{align}
Estimate or truncate the correlation sum statistically. Effective sample
counts depend on the observable; finer output stride does not add independent
information over a fixed duration.

Block errors should plateau for blocks longer than the correlation time,
while retaining enough blocks \cite{flyvbjerg1989}. Independent replicas
help detect slow modes, but neither method reveals a basin never visited.
Resample whole replicas or sufficiently long blocks and recompute the full
estimator, preserving cross-temperature correlations. Variance-based heat
capacities require errors of squared fluctuations, not merely errors of the
mean enthalpy. Sampling error, finite-size effects, integration bias, and
potential error need separate checks.

\subsubsection{Configurational populations}

For temperature-independent basins $\mathcal B_a$,
\begin{equation}
  \Delta=\sum_a\Delta_a,\qquad
  w_a=\frac{\Delta_a}{\Delta},\qquad
  \langle H\rangle=\sum_a w_a\langle H\rangle_a.
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  C_P=\sum_a w_a\frac{\partial\langle H\rangle_a}{\partial T}
      +\sum_a\langle H\rangle_a\frac{\partial w_a}{\partial T}.
\end{equation}
The two terms describe motion within basins and redistribution between them.
A Hessian characterizes one local environment; adequately sampled MD also
captures changing guest arrangements and framework conformations. Basin
weights depend on accessible phase-space volume, so counting optimized
structures does not establish equilibrium populations.

\subsection{Classical molecular dynamics}
\label{sec:classical-md-theory}

MD estimates equilibrium averages by integrating forces, with periodic
boundaries and reservoir couplings appropriate to the ensemble.

\subsubsection{Periodic boundaries and pressure}

Periodic images differ by $\bm h\bm n$, $\bm n\in\mathbb Z^3$. A
single-image cutoff requires $r_{\mathrm c}$ below half the shortest
nonzero lattice-vector length throughout cell deformation; otherwise include
all images within the cutoff. Skew cells require a geometric nearest-image
search. Neighbor lists extend to $r_{\mathrm c}+r_{\mathrm{skin}}$ and must
be rebuilt before omitted pairs can enter the cutoff. Smooth cutoffs avoid
force jumps. Separate electrostatic terms, if present, need converged
long-range treatment consistent with the potential.

For unconstrained atoms with bulk streaming motion removed,
\begin{equation}
  \bm P_{\mathrm{inst}}
  =\frac{1}{V}\left[\sum_i\frac{\bm p_i\otimes\bm p_i}{m_i}
                         +\bm W\right],\qquad
  P_{\mathrm{inst}}=\frac{1}{3}\operatorname{tr}\bm P_{\mathrm{inst}}.
  \label{eq:pressure-virial}
\end{equation}
Here $\otimes$ denotes an outer product. Pair forces give
$\bm W=\sum_{i<j}\bm r_{ij}\otimes\bm F_{ij}$; many-body potentials use
strain derivatives at fixed fractional positions:
\begin{equation}
  W_{\alpha\beta}
  =-\left.\frac{\partial U}{\partial\epsilon_{\alpha\beta}}
    \right|_{\bm\epsilon=0,\{\bm s_i\}},\qquad
  \bm h\mapsto(\bm I+\bm\epsilon)\bm h.
\end{equation}
Include constraint and long-range contributions when present. Tensile-positive
stress has the opposite sign to pressure; model virial omits the kinetic
contribution to full pressure \cite{lammps-pressure}.

\subsubsection{Time integration}

Fixed-cell velocity Verlet updates momenta and positions as
\begin{align}
  \bm p_i^{n+1/2}
    &=\bm p_i^n+\frac{\Delta t}{2}\bm F_i(\bm R^n),\\
  \bm R_i^{n+1}
    &=\bm R_i^n+\Delta t\,\frac{\bm p_i^{n+1/2}}{m_i},\\
  \bm p_i^{n+1}
    &=\bm p_i^{n+1/2}+\frac{\Delta t}{2}\bm F_i(\bm R^{n+1}).
  \label{eq:velocity-verlet}
\end{align}
It is reversible, symplectic, and second order, requiring one new force
evaluation per step. The harmonic stability condition
$\omega_{\max}\Delta t<2$ does not ensure accurate sampling: converge the
timestep using energy drift in short NVE tests and the target observables.
Thermostats can conceal integration errors. This report retains flexible
bonds; constraints would change the heat capacity and require matching
harmonic mode bookkeeping.

\subsubsection{Thermostats and barostats}

Langevin dynamics samples the canonical distribution through balanced
friction and Gaussian noise:
\begin{align}
  \dot{\bm p}_i&=\bm F_i-\gamma\bm p_i+\bm\eta_i(t),\\
  \langle\eta_{i\alpha}(t)\eta_{j\beta}(t')\rangle
    &=2m_i\gamma k_{\mathrm B}T\,
      \delta_{ij}\delta_{\alpha\beta}\delta(t-t').
  \label{eq:langevin}
\end{align}
Here $\gamma$ is an inverse coupling time. Nos\'e--Hoover instead uses
an auxiliary friction variable:
\begin{equation}
  \dot{\bm p}_i=\bm F_i-\xi\bm p_i,\qquad
  \dot\xi=\frac{2K-fk_{\mathrm B}T}{Q},
\end{equation}
The inertia $Q\sim f k_{\mathrm B}T\tau_T^2$ sets the response time.
Chains improve sampling of nearly harmonic systems \cite{nhc}, while
simple weak coupling or exact velocity rescaling can suppress required
fluctuations.

A barostat changes volume or cell strain. Flexible-cell MTK algorithms
include the measure corrections required for NPT \cite{mtk}. Overly rapid
coupling excites cell motion; slow coupling limits exploration. Converge
coupling times and check pressure averages and fluctuations. Thermostatted
trajectories yield equilibrium properties, but transport coefficients also
require a validated dynamical protocol.

\subsection{Graph theory for neural networks and sparse differentiation}
\label{sec:graph-theory}

Graphs describe atomic neighborhoods and derivative dependencies
\cite{diestel2025,bronstein2021}. Their roles are distinguished in
Table~\ref{tab:graph-roles}.

\subsubsection{Adjacency, paths, and receptive fields}

A graph $\mathcal G=(\mathcal V,\mathcal E)$ contains vertices and edges.
Atomic graphs are directed periodic multigraphs: messages have directions,
and repeated images can connect the same atom labels. Edges indicate
interactions, not necessarily chemical bonds. For a simple undirected graph,
\begin{equation}
  A_{ij}=\begin{cases}
    1,&\{i,j\}\in\mathcal E,\\
    0,&\text{otherwise},
  \end{cases}
  \qquad \mathcal N_i=\{j:A_{ij}=1\}.
\end{equation}
The degree is $d_i=|\mathcal N_i|$. Species and displacements add node and
edge attributes; Boolean adjacency alone loses geometry and image multiplicity.

Graph distance counts edges in a shortest path. Its $k$-hop ball is
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal B_k(i)=\{j:d_{\mathcal G}(i,j)\leq k\}.
\end{equation}
For $1-2-3-4$, $\mathcal B_1(1)=\{1,2\}$ and
$\mathcal B_2(1)=\{1,2,3\}$. Boolean powers of adjacency record
reachability (Eq.~\eqref{eq:hop-pattern}). With $L$ local message-passing
layers, a node depends on its $L$-hop neighborhood. Atoms influencing the
same nonlinear readout can have mixed second derivatives beyond one hop.

Relabeling by $\bm P$ transforms adjacency to $\bm P\bm A\bm P^{\mathrm T}$.
Node outputs must permute consistently; total energy must remain invariant
(Eq.~\eqref{eq:graph-permutation}). The computation graph instead records
operations and directed dependencies. Forward evaluation/AD follows its
acyclic order; reverse AD applies the chain rule backward \cite{baydin2018}.

\subsubsection{Coloring derivative directions}

A sparsity pattern $S_{pq}=1$ permits dependence of output $p$ on input $q$.
Columns sharing a possible nonzero row are adjacent in the
column-intersection graph. Proper coloring assigns them different colors,
so same-color columns have disjoint supports and can share an AD seed
\cite{hill2025asd}.
\begin{equation}
  \bm M=\begin{pmatrix}
    a&b&0&0\\ b&c&d&0\\ 0&d&e&f\\ 0&0&f&g
  \end{pmatrix}.
  \label{eq:graph-coloring-example}
\end{equation}
For this tridiagonal matrix, alternating chain colors $(1,2,1,2)$ fail:
columns 1 and 3 both contribute to row 2. Colors $(1,2,3,1)$ allow
columns 1 and 4 to share a seed:
\begin{equation}
  \bm M(1,0,0,1)^{\mathrm T}=(a,b,f,g)^{\mathrm T}.
\end{equation}
Three products then recover the matrix instead of four.

A \emph{star coloring} of the symmetric sparsity graph requires at least
three colors on every four-vertex path; every two-color subgraph is a union
of stars. The chain coloring $(1,2,1,3)$ satisfies this condition. Hessian
symmetry provides a second compressed location for each off-diagonal entry,
allowing some overlapping supports. SADMOF uses this recovery strategy
\cite{gebremedhin2005,sadMof}; Section~\ref{sec:asd-method} gives the formulas.
Either strategy is exact only when the pattern includes every actual coupling.

\subsection{Machine-learned interatomic potentials}
\label{sec:mlip-theory}

\subsubsection{Energy, forces, and atomic graphs}

An MLIP approximates the Born--Oppenheimer map
$(\bm R,\bm h,\bm Z)\mapsto U$ with fitted parameters $\theta$.
A local, extensive decomposition is
\begin{equation}
  U_\theta(\bm R,\bm h,\bm Z)
  = U_{\mathrm{base}}(\bm Z)
  + \sum_{i=1}^{N}\varepsilon_i(\mathcal N_i;\theta),
  \label{eq:mlip-energy}
\end{equation}
The optional baseline depends only on composition. Atomic energies are model
contributions, not unique observables. At bounded neighbor count, evaluation
cost scales approximately linearly with atom count. Differentiating the
same scalar energy gives conservative forces and a Cartesian Hessian:
\begin{equation}
  \bm F_i=-\frac{\partial U_\theta}{\partial \bm R_i},
  \label{eq:mlip-force}
\end{equation}

Atoms are graph vertices with species features. Periodic directed edges
$(v,u,\bm n)$ carry relative geometry:
\begin{align}
  \bm r_{uv\bm n}
    &= \bm R_v+\bm h\bm n-\bm R_u,
  \\
  r_{uv\bm n}&=\lVert\bm r_{uv\bm n}\rVert < r_{\mathrm c},
  \label{eq:periodic-edge}
\end{align}
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal N_u
  =\{(v,\bm n):(v,u,\bm n)\in\mathcal E\}.
  \label{eq:periodic-neighborhood}
\end{equation}
Retaining image labels distinguishes repeated neighbors. Species embeddings
and radial edge features provide numerical inputs:
\begin{equation}
  \bm x_u^{(0)}=\bm E_Z[Z_u]\in\mathbb R^d,
  \label{eq:species-embedding}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  \bm x_{uv\bm n}^{\mathrm{edge}}
  =f_{\mathrm c}(r_{uv\bm n})
   \bigl[\varphi_1(r_{uv\bm n}),\ldots,
          \varphi_K(r_{uv\bm n})\bigr],
  \label{eq:radial-embedding}
\end{equation}
Directional models also use displacement vectors or their learned functions;
latent feature vectors need not transform as physical vectors.

Figures~\ref{fig:cutoff-single} and \ref{fig:cutoff-functions} illustrate
cutoff weighting. Smooth forces and Hessians require consistent derivatives
through attention normalization and readout as well as radial features.

Node-wise $F$ and scalar readout $f$ obey
\begin{align}
  F(\bm P\bm X,\bm P\bm A\bm P^{\mathrm T})
    &=\bm P F(\bm X,\bm A),
  \\
  f(\bm P\bm X,\bm P\bm A\bm P^{\mathrm T})
    &=f(\bm X,\bm A).
  \label{eq:graph-permutation}
\end{align}
Relabel edge attributes and periodic images consistently.

\subsubsection{Message passing}

Each layer combines incoming messages with a symmetric aggregation
$\bigoplus$ (usually a sum), then updates the node representation
\cite{bronstein2021}:
\begin{equation}
  \bm h_u
  =\phi\!\left(
      \bm x_u,
      \bigoplus_{(v,\bm n)\in\mathcal N_u}
      \psi\!\left(\bm x_u,\bm x_v,
                   \bm x_{uv\bm n}^{\mathrm{edge}}\right)
    \right).
  \label{eq:message-passing}
\end{equation}
Repeating the update through $L$ layers creates many-body features over an
$L$-hop receptive field. A shared readout
$\varepsilon_u=\rho(\bm h_u^{(L)})$ gives the total energy through
Eq.~\eqref{eq:mlip-energy}.

\subsubsection{Point Edge Transformer architecture}
\label{sec:pet-architecture}

PET retains distinct directed-edge messages while attention mixes all tokens
in one atomic environment (Figure~\ref{fig:pet-architecture}) \cite{pet}.
It underlies PET-MAD \cite{petmad}. In the original formulation,
\begin{align}
  \bm p_{ij}^{(\ell)}
    &=\operatorname{SiLU}\bigl(\bm W_r^{(\ell)}\bm r_{ij}
                                      +\bm b_r^{(\ell)}\bigr),
  \\
  \bm t_{ij}^{(\ell)}
    &=\mathrm{MLP}_\ell\!\left(
      \bm m_{j\to i}^{(\ell-1)}\Vert
      \bm E_n^{(\ell)}[Z_j]\Vert
      \bm p_{ij}^{(\ell)}\right),
  \label{eq:pet-token}
\end{align}
Here $\Vert$ concatenates the incoming message, neighbor embedding, and
position encoding. The MLP maps $3d_{\mathrm{PET}}$ features to
$d_{\mathrm{PET}}$; the first block omits the incoming message. A separate
central token is $\bm t_{i0}^{(\ell)}=\bm E_c^{(\ell)}[Z_i]$.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/pet_final.pdf}
  \caption{Point Edge Transformer (PET) message-passing architecture. Edge
  messages associated with a central atom are jointly processed by attention
  while retaining their individual edge identities for the outgoing update.
  Figure from the PET paper \cite{pet}.}
  \label{fig:pet-architecture}
\end{figure}

For one attention head,
\begin{align}
  \bm q_a&=\bm W_Q\bm t_a,
  &\bm k_b&=\bm W_K\bm t_b,
  \\
  \bm v_b&=\bm W_V\bm t_b,
  \\
  \alpha_{ab}
    &=\frac{\exp(\bm q_a^{\mathrm T}\bm k_b/\sqrt{d_k})
                    c_b}
            {\sum_c\exp(\bm q_a^{\mathrm T}\bm k_c/\sqrt{d_k})
                    c_c},
  \\
  \bm y_a&=\sum_b\alpha_{ab}\bm v_b.
  \label{eq:self-attention}
\end{align}
With $c_0=1$ and $c_j=f_{\mathrm c}(r_{ij})$, departing neighbors vanish
from both numerator and normalization. Multi-head attention, feed-forward
layers, and residual connections preserve neighbor permutation equivariance.
Attention layers enrich interactions within one environment; message-passing
blocks extend the receptive field. Local attention cost is quadratic in
neighbor count, but total cost remains approximately linear in atom count
at fixed architecture and bounded neighborhoods.

Outgoing edge tokens update the messages. The original summed-edge readout is
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  U_\theta={}&\sum_i b_{Z_i}
  +\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\sum_i\Bigl[
      H_c^{(\ell)}(\bm x_{i0}^{(\ell)})
  \\[-0.2em]
  &+\sum_{j\in\mathcal N_i}f_{\mathrm c}(r_{ij})
                 H_n^{(\ell)}(\bm x_{ij}^{(\ell)})\Bigr],
  \end{aligned}
  \label{eq:pet-readout}
\end{equation}
$H_c$ and $H_n$ are learned heads, $\bm x$ are output tokens, and $b_Z$
are species baselines. Every block contributes; cutoff weighting also enters
the readout. Edge contributions are learned many-body terms, not pair energies.
Project-specific dimensions appear in Section~\ref{sec:pet-implementation}.

\subsubsection{Physical symmetries and ECSE}

Scalar energies should respect translation, rotation/reflection, and
identical-atom permutation:
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  U(\bm R)&=U(\bm R+\bm a)=U(\bm Q\bm R)
  \\
           &=U(\bm P\bm R),\qquad \bm Q\in O(3).
  \end{aligned}
  \label{eq:energy-symmetry}
\end{equation}
Forces then transform equivariantly,
$\bm F_i(\bm Q\bm R)=\bm Q\bm F_i(\bm R)$; periodic tests rotate both
positions and cell. PET enforces translation and permutation symmetries,
but learns rotational behavior through augmentation. Reported rotational
errors below prediction errors \cite{petmad} do not validate this checkpoint
on loaded MOF-5.

The optional Equivariant Coordinate System Ensemble (ECSE) imposes exact
rotation symmetry through local frames from ordered noncollinear neighbor
pairs \cite{pet}:

\begin{figure}[htbp]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/ecse_final.pdf}
  \caption{Equivariant Coordinate System Ensemble (ECSE). Local coordinate
  frames constructed from noncollinear neighbor pairs rotate with the atomic
  environment; predictions evaluated in these frames are transformed as
  needed and smoothly averaged. Figure from the PET paper \cite{pet}.}
  \label{fig:ecse}
\end{figure}

\begin{equation}
  \varepsilon_i^{\mathrm{ECSE}}=
    \frac{\sum_{j\ne k}w_{ijk}\,
          \varepsilon_i^0(\bm Q_{ijk}^{\mathrm T}\mathcal N_i)}
         {\sum_{j\ne k}w_{ijk}},
  \label{eq:ecse}
\end{equation}
$\bm Q_{ijk}^{\mathrm T}\mathcal N_i$ expresses displacements in a
co-rotating frame; vector/tensor outputs are transformed back before averaging.
These proper $SO(3)$ rotations alone do not enforce reflection invariance.
Smooth radial/angular weights suppress cutoff and nearly collinear frames;
fully collinear environments need a fallback. The full frame and weight
dependence must be differentiated for forces and Hessians. Tight frame
selection can amplify derivative errors, and the proof-of-principle overhead
is roughly $10^3$ \cite{pet}. ECSE is not enabled here.

\subsubsection{Training and validity}

A generic energy--force objective is
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  \mathcal L(\theta)=\sum_s\Biggl[{}
    w_E\left(\frac{U_\theta^{(s)}-U_{\mathrm{ref}}^{(s)}}{N_s}\right)^2
  \\
    +\frac{w_F}{3N_s}\sum_{i\alpha}
      \left(F_{i\alpha,\theta}^{(s)}
                 -F_{i\alpha,\mathrm{ref}}^{(s)}\right)^2
  \Biggr],
  \end{aligned}
  \label{eq:mlip-loss}
\end{equation}
Stress terms and regularization may be added. PET's original loss uses a
different normalization with moving validation errors, species baselines,
Adam, and rotational augmentation \cite{pet}. PET-MAD is pretrained on a
chemically diverse DFT dataset \cite{petmad}.

An MLIP inherits its reference method's approximations and may extrapolate
poorly to unfamiliar environments. Hold-out errors, trajectory stability,
and uncertainty indicators test different aspects of validity. Curvatures
need explicit validation because the usual loss does not constrain Hessians
directly; PET's methane benchmarks do not establish accuracy for adsorption
in MOF-5.

\subsection{Thermodynamic definitions and uncertainty}

The classical term differentiates physical NPT enthalpy at fixed loading and
pressure. The harmonic correction is formally $C_V$; adding it to $C_P$
assumes a small quantum pressure--volume contribution
(Eq.~\eqref{eq:cp-cv}). Combine extensive terms before dividing by loaded mass.

\subsubsection{LLPR model uncertainty and CEA propagation}
\label{sec:llpr-cea}

Finite-trajectory sampling error is distinct from potential uncertainty.
LLPR varies the final readout of a fixed PET representation \cite{bigi2024}.
For central energy $\bar U=\bm w_0^{\mathrm T}\bm f(\bm x)$ and
covariance-set features $\bm F$,
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  \sigma_{\mathrm{LLPR}}^2(\bm x)
  ={}&\alpha^2\bm f(\bm x)^{\mathrm T}
  \\
  &\times\left(\bm F^{\mathrm T}\bm F
        +\lambda\bm I\right)^{-1}\bm f(\bm x).
  \end{aligned}
  \label{eq:llpr-variance}
\end{equation}
The regularizer $\lambda$ stabilizes weakly constrained directions; $\alpha$
is calibrated against energy residuals on a disjoint reference-labeled set
using the base potential's electronic-structure definition. Predictions
cannot calibrate their own error.

The exported \texttt{energy\_\allowbreak{}uncertainty} is an energy standard deviation.
Property propagation instead needs persistent, signed energy variations:
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  \bm w_i&\sim\mathcal N\!\left[
    \bm w_0,
    \alpha^2\left(\bm F^{\mathrm T}\bm F
                         +\lambda\bm I\right)^{-1}\right],
  \\
  U^{(i)}(\bm x)&=\bm w_i^{\mathrm T}\bm f(\bm x),
  \end{aligned}
  \label{eq:llpr-members}
\end{equation}
The exported \texttt{energy\_\allowbreak{}ensemble} preserves member identity across
configurations. Its large-ensemble spread approaches the analytical LLPR
value, but cannot capture bias shared by the frozen representation.

Following Imbalzano \emph{et al.}\ \cite{imbalzano2021}, central-trajectory
frames supply
\begin{align}
  \Delta U_t^{(i)} &= U_t^{(i)}-\bar U_t,\\
  H_t^{(i)} &= K_t+U_t^{(i)}+P_{\mathrm{ext}}V_t,
  \label{eq:llpr-frame-enthalpy}
\end{align}
\begin{equation}
  \left\langle H^{(i)}\right\rangle_i^{\mathrm{direct}}
  =\frac{\sum_t e^{-\beta\Delta U_t^{(i)}}H_t^{(i)}}
  {\sum_t e^{-\beta\Delta U_t^{(i)}}},
  \label{eq:llpr-direct}
\end{equation}
Extensive energy differences can collapse the exponential weights. The
primary large-system estimate uses first-order cumulant expansion (CEA):
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  \left\langle H^{(i)}\right\rangle_i^{\mathrm{CEA}}
  ={}&\left\langle H^{(i)}\right\rangle_{\bar U}
  \\
  &-\beta\operatorname{cov}_{\bar U}
  \left(H^{(i)},\Delta U^{(i)}\right),
  \\
  &\beta=(k_{\mathrm B}T)^{-1}.
  \end{aligned}
  \label{eq:llpr-cea}
\end{equation}
Direct reweighting remains a diagnostic:
$N_{\mathrm{eff}}=1/\sum_t\widetilde w_t^2$ should remain adequate, and
$\operatorname{var}_t(\beta\Delta U_t^{(i)})\ll1$ is needed for controlled
first-order CEA.

Average replicas member by member, preserve indices across temperatures,
and differentiate each complete enthalpy curve:
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  C_{P,\mathrm{cl}}^{(i)}(T)
  &=\frac{\mathrm d\langle H^{(i)}\rangle_i^{\mathrm{CEA}}}
          {\mathrm dT},
  \\
  \sigma_{\mathrm{LLPR},C_P}(T)
  &=\left\{\frac{1}{M-1}\sum_{i=1}^{M}
  \left[C_{P,\mathrm{cl}}^{(i)}(T)
  -\overline C_{P,\mathrm{cl}}(T)\right]^2\right\}^{1/2}.
  \end{aligned}
  \label{eq:llpr-cp-spread}
\end{equation}
The final property spread is a predictive standard deviation; do not divide
it by $\sqrt M$.

\subsubsection{Gaussian NPT propagation}
\label{sec:gaussian-propagation}

Direct propagation of shallow ensembles (DPOSE) combines member properties
before measuring their spread \cite{kellner2024}. The saved comparison uses
a Gaussian NPT approximation on central-model trajectories. Define
$Q_i=U_i+P_{\mathrm{ext}}V$ and $\Delta U_i=U_i-U_0$.
Joint Gaussianity leaves covariance unchanged under exponential reweighting,
giving
\begin{equation}
 C_{P,i}^{\mathrm G}\simeq\frac{f}{2}k_{\mathrm B}
       +\frac{\operatorname{var}_0(Q_i)}{k_{\mathrm B}T^2}.
 \label{eq:gaussian-member-cp}
\end{equation}
Here $f=3N-3$. The central-model fluctuation estimator is exact under the
usual equilibrium assumptions; using central-trajectory variances for other
members needs Gaussian closure. Pair member indices with harmonic corrections.

The main Gaussian plots replace only the propagated model spread around the
central enthalpy-derivative curve. The separate variance-based hybrid also
replaces the central classical value and estimates sampling error from
squared configurational-enthalpy fluctuations. Neither guarantees smaller
or better calibrated uncertainty.

\subsubsection{Classical versus quantum vibrations}

Classical harmonic modes contribute $k_{\mathrm B}$ each at every temperature.
Quantum occupation decreases when $\hbar\omega\geq k_{\mathrm B}T$
(Eq.~\eqref{eq:quantum-harmonic-cv}). Longer classical trajectories cannot
recover that suppression.

\subsection{Path integral molecular dynamics}
\label{sec:pimd-theory}

PIMD samples equilibrium quantum nuclei on the full anharmonic potential.
It is a prospective extension; present results use classical MD plus a
harmonic correction. The following assumes distinguishable nuclei and
neglects exchange permutations \cite{tuckerman2023}.

\subsubsection{Ring-polymer mapping}

At fixed cell,
\begin{equation}
  Z_{\mathrm q}=\operatorname{Tr}e^{-\beta\hat{\mathcal H}},\qquad
  \hat{\mathcal H}=\sum_i\frac{\hat{\bm p}_i^2}{2m_i}
                         +U(\hat{\bm R};\bm h).
\end{equation}
Noncommuting kinetic and potential operators are split into $L$ imaginary-time
slices, $\beta_L=\beta/L$:
\begin{equation}
  e^{-\beta\hat{\mathcal H}}
  =\lim_{L\to\infty}
   \left[e^{-\beta_L\hat U/2}
         e^{-\beta_L\hat K}
         e^{-\beta_L\hat U/2}\right]^L.
\end{equation}
Inserting position states gives
\begin{align}
  Z_L&=\mathcal A_L\int\prod_{j=1}^{L}\mathrm d^{3N}R^{(j)}\,
                   e^{-\beta U_L^{\mathrm{eff}}},\\
  \mathcal A_L&=\prod_{i=1}^{N}
           \left(\frac{m_iL}{2\pi\beta\hbar^2}\right)^{3L/2},\\
  U_L^{\mathrm{eff}}
    &=\frac{1}{L}\sum_{j=1}^{L}U(\bm R^{(j)};\bm h)\notag\\
    &\quad+\frac{1}{L}\sum_{i,j}\frac{m_i\omega_L^2}{2}
           |\bm R_i^{(j)}-\bm R_i^{(j+1)}|^2,
       \label{eq:ring-polymer-potential}\\
  \omega_L&=\frac{L}{\beta\hbar},\qquad
  \bm R_i^{(L+1)}=\bm R_i^{(1)}.
\end{align}
Each nucleus becomes a closed ring of $L$ beads connected by springs; the
physical potential acts within each slice. Beads resolve imaginary time,
not a real trajectory. Periodic paths need consistent images and, where
relevant, winding sectors. The equivalent convention samples
$L U_L^{\mathrm{eff}}$ at $LT$. At $L=1$ the classical configuration
weight is recovered; the symmetric primitive error is $\mathcal O(L^{-2})$
for smooth potentials \cite{tuckerman2023,ceriotti2010pimd}.

\subsubsection{Sampling and observables}

Auxiliary momenta sample
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal H_{\mathrm{samp}}
   =\sum_{i,j}\frac{|\bm\pi_i^{(j)}|^2}{2\mu_i^{(j)}}
    +U_L^{\mathrm{eff}}.
\end{equation}
Fictitious masses $\mu$ affect efficiency; physical masses $m$ in the
springs determine quantum delocalization. Normal-mode thermostats such as
PILE handle stiff springs \cite{ceriotti2010pimd}. PIMD simulation time is
only a sampling variable; quantum transport or rates require additional
real-time approximations such as RPMD/CMD.

For position-diagonal observables,
\begin{equation}
  \langle A\rangle_{\mathrm q}
  =\lim_{L\to\infty}\left\langle
      \frac{1}{L}\sum_{j=1}^{L}A(\bm R^{(j)})\right\rangle_L,
\end{equation}
A nonlinear observable at the centroid generally differs from the bead
average. A centroid-virial physical energy estimator is
\begin{align}
  \overline{\bm R}_i&=\frac{1}{L}\sum_j\bm R_i^{(j)},\\
  K_{\mathrm{cv}}&=\frac{3N}{2\beta}
      -\frac{1}{2L}\sum_{i,j}
       (\bm R_i^{(j)}-\overline{\bm R}_i)\cdot\bm F_i^{(j)},\\
  E_{\mathrm{est}}&=K_{\mathrm{cv}}
       +\frac{1}{L}\sum_j U(\bm R^{(j)};\bm h),
       \label{eq:pimd-energy-estimator}
\end{align}
Use physical forces and unwrapped paths, with matching modifications for
constraints or removed degrees of freedom \cite{yamamoto2005}.
NPT also requires a shared cell, quantum pressure estimator, and correct
cell measure \cite{ceriotti2014ipi}:
\begin{align}
  \langle H\rangle_{\mathrm q}
    &=\lim_{L\to\infty}\langle E_{\mathrm{est}}+P_{\mathrm{ext}}V\rangle_L,\\
  C_P^{\mathrm q}
    &=\left(\frac{\partial\langle H\rangle_{\mathrm q}}{\partial T}
      \right)_{N,P_{\mathrm{ext}}}.
      \label{eq:pimd-cp}
\end{align}
Exclude fictitious kinetic and spring energies from physical enthalpy.
A mean-energy estimator's variance is not automatically the quantum
enthalpy variance; direct heat-capacity estimators must differentiate the
action, normalization, and estimator \cite{yamamoto2005}.

\subsubsection{Prospective validation}

Converge beads at every derivative temperature, starting with
$\beta\hbar\omega_{\max}/L\ll1$. Each step costs approximately $L$ force
evaluations. An i-PI/PET interface \cite{ipi-documentation} should first
recover $L=1$ classical results, then stable quantum harmonic benchmarks,
before loaded-MOF NPT tests. Converge timestep, thermostats, cell size, and
sampling as well as beads; validate quantum-displaced configurations against
the potential's domain. Converged PIMD would test the hybrid's missing quantum
anharmonic and pressure--volume terms. Do not add a harmonic quantum
correction again to PIMD results.

\section{Reference evidence and the hybrid approximation}

Kapil \emph{et al.}\ tested empty MOF-5 and 50-, 100-, and 150-methane cells
\cite{kapil2019}. Their hybrid strategy motivates this project; its accuracy
on PET remains to be established.

\subsection{Differences from the reference calculation}
\label{sec:reference-comparison}

The reference used QuickFF \cite{quickff}: B3LYP cluster-based bonded terms,
MBIS charges from periodic PBE, Gaussian electrostatics, and MM3 Buckingham
interactions with a \SI{15}{\angstrom} cutoff and tail corrections.
RASPA insertion and canonical Monte Carlo preceded MD. Here PET supplies all
energies, forces, and Hessians after independent random guest placement.

Both workflows differentiate neighboring equilibrium enthalpies:
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  C_P(T)&\simeq
  \frac{\langle H\rangle_{T+\Delta T}-\langle H\rangle_{T-\Delta T}}
       {2\Delta T},
  \\
  \Delta T&=\SI{25}{K},
  \end{aligned}
  \label{eq:finite-difference-cp}
\end{equation}
The reference used optimized Yaff Hessians and TAMkin modes, compared QuickFF
and UFF4MOF for the empty host, and optimized five Monte Carlo configurations
for the 100-methane harmonic calculation.

\subsection{Physical conclusions}
\label{sec:reference-conclusions}

The reference found nearly harmonic, quantum-mechanical empty MOF-5
(roughly \SI{0.7}{J\per g\per K} near room temperature). Classical
occupations overestimate its heat capacity. Methane introduces anharmonic
host--guest motion and a loading-dependent minimum near \SI{200}{K},
associated with changing attractive interactions as guests become mobile.
The combination
\begin{equation}
          C \approx C_{\mathrm{qn}}^{\mathrm{har}}
          - C_{\mathrm{cl}}^{\mathrm{har}}
          + C_{\mathrm{cl}}^{\mathrm{anh}}
          \label{eq:quantum-correction}
        \end{equation}
reproduced the minimum qualitatively and was useful above about \SI{200}{K}
in their 100-methane test. A loaded-system spectrum is essential: the empty
Hessian omits guest modes and host--guest coupling. Equation~\eqref{eq:hybrid-cp}
gives the operational PET estimator.

\subsection{Harmonic approximation and Cartesian Hessian}

Let $\bm R_0$ be a stationary reference structure and
$\bm u=\bm R-\bm R_0$. Expanding the potential energy gives
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  U(\bm R_0+\bm u)={}&U(\bm R_0)
  +\underbrace{\nabla U(\bm R_0)\cdot\bm u}
       _{=0\ \mathrm{at\ a\ minimum}}
  \\
  &+\frac{1}{2}\bm u^{\mathrm T}\bm H\bm u
  +\mathcal O(u^3),
  \end{aligned}
  \label{eq:taylor}
\end{equation}
with Cartesian Hessian
\begin{equation}
  H_{i\alpha,j\beta}
  =\frac{\partial^2 U}{\partial R_{i\alpha}\partial R_{j\beta}}
  =-\frac{\partial F_{i\alpha}}{\partial R_{j\beta}}.
  \label{eq:hessian}
\end{equation}
For $N$ atoms, $\bm H$ has dimensions $3N\times3N$; for the 100-methane cell
this is $2772\times2772$.

\subsubsection{Automatic differentiation}

At fixed parameters, a neural-network energy composes differentiable operations:
\begin{equation}
  \bm z^{(0)}=\bm R,\qquad
  \bm z^{(\ell+1)}=\phi_\ell(\bm z^{(\ell)};\theta),\qquad
  U_\theta=\rho(\bm z^{(L)}).
  \label{eq:nn-computational-graph}
\end{equation}
AD applies their chain rules without a finite-displacement step
\cite{baydin2018}. It differentiates the implemented potential, up to
floating-point error; it does not improve the potential's accuracy.
For $\bm f:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R^m$,
\begin{equation}
  (\bm J_f)_{pq}=\frac{\partial f_p}{\partial x_q},
  \qquad \bm J_f\in\mathbb R^{m\times n}.
  \label{eq:jacobian-definition}
\end{equation}
Forward AD evaluates $\bm J_f\bm v$ (JVP); reverse AD evaluates
$\bm w^{\mathrm T}\bm J_f$ (VJP). Dense recovery requires $n$ forward
or $m$ reverse seeds, without storing layer Jacobians \cite{hill2025asd}.
A single reverse sweep yields $\nabla U$ and hence all forces.
Forward-over-reverse evaluates
\begin{equation}
  \bm H\bm v=
  \left.\frac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm d\epsilon}
  \nabla_{\bm R}U_\theta(\bm R+\epsilon\bm v)
  \right|_{\epsilon=0}.
  \label{eq:hessian-vector-product}
\end{equation}
Dense Hessian recovery needs $3N$ such products. Sparse reconstruction reduces
the seed count; cutoff smoothness and neighbor-selection conventions still
control which function is differentiated.

\subsubsection{Sparse Hessian reconstruction with SADMOF}
\label{sec:asd-method}

SADMOF constructs an atom-pair pattern from PET's selected-neighbor graph,
then expands each pair into a Cartesian $3\times3$ block \cite{sadMof}.
For $L$ message-passing layers, the maximum Hessian reach is $K=2L$ for
node readout and $K=2L+1$ for PET edge readout. The paper's MACE-MP-0 medium,
PET-XS, and PET-S reaches are four, five, and seven hops. Boolean adjacency gives
\begin{equation}
  \bm P^{(k)}=\bm I\lor\bm A\lor\bm A^2\lor\cdots\lor\bm A^k,
  \label{eq:hop-pattern}
\end{equation}
At $k=K$ the pattern contains all couplings under the chosen derivative
convention; $k<K$ truncates it. Additional nonlocal terms need separate analysis.
Structural sparsity requires
\begin{equation}
  S_{pq}=0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad H_{pq}=0
  \label{eq:hessian-sparsity-pattern}
\end{equation}
An overestimate costs entries and colors. An underestimate can both discard
curvature and contaminate retained entries. Mixed second derivatives depend
on nonlinear readout, not just direct interaction edges.

For direct column recovery, connect columns with overlapping supports:
\begin{equation}
  (q,r)\in\mathcal E_S
  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
  \exists p:\ S_{pq}=S_{pr}=1.
  \label{eq:column-intersection-graph}
\end{equation}
A proper coloring makes same-color columns structurally orthogonal.
Greedy heuristics need not minimize color count to recover exact entries
\cite{hill2025asd}. Define one seed per color class,
\begin{equation}
  \bm v_a=\sum_{q\in\mathcal C_a}\bm e_q,
  \qquad
  \bm H\bm v_a=\sum_{q\in\mathcal C_a}\bm H\bm e_q.
  \label{eq:colored-hvp}
\end{equation}
and collect seeds in $\bm V\in\{0,1\}^{3N\times n_c}$:
\begin{equation}
  \bm Y=\bm H\bm V,
  \qquad
  Y_{pa}=\sum_{q:c(q)=a}H_{pq}.
  \label{eq:compressed-hessian}
\end{equation}
Disjoint supports permit
\begin{equation}
  H_{pq}=Y_{p,c(q)}\qquad\text{when }S_{pq}=1,
  \label{eq:hessian-decompression}
\end{equation}
with zeros outside the pattern.

ASDEX instead star-colors the symmetric Hessian graph. For each pair
$\{p,q\}$, a hub--spoke orientation selects an uncontaminated compressed
location and writes it to both $H_{pq}$ and $H_{qp}$. This can reduce colors
\cite{hill2025asd,sadMof}. Atom-block coloring can be lifted to coordinate
colors $3c_i+d$, $d\in\{0,1,2\}$; availability depends on the installed
ASDEX implementation.

The pipeline is summarized by
\begin{equation}
  \bm S\ \xrightarrow{\mathrm{color}}\ \bm V
  \ \xrightarrow{\mathrm{HVPs}}\ \bm Y=\bm H\bm V
  \ \xrightarrow{\mathrm{recover}}\ \bm H,
  \qquad \text{ideal AD reduction}=\frac{3N}{n_c}.
\end{equation}
Pattern setup, compilation, decompression, and GPU utilization limit the
wall-time gain. Reuse a coloring only while its structural pattern remains valid.

The wrapper uses \texttt{atoms\_\allowbreak{}to\_\allowbreak{}inputs}, \texttt{sparsity\_\allowbreak{}pattern},
\texttt{asdex.\allowbreak{}hessian\_\allowbreak{}coloring\_\allowbreak{}from\_\allowbreak{}sparsity},
\texttt{get\_\allowbreak{}energy\_\allowbreak{}fn}, \texttt{get\_\allowbreak{}hessian\_\allowbreak{}fn}, and \texttt{jax.\allowbreak{}jit}.
It densifies the sparse result and removes padding before eigendecomposition;
only the AD stage exploits sparsity.

\texttt{no\_\allowbreak{}shadow=True} stops adaptive-neighbor-selection derivatives and
uses the selected graph. Shadow derivatives introduce extra couplings;
\texttt{shadow=true} is rejected in this sparse path. Check consistency
with MD forces. With three PET blocks, the paper derives $K=7$, so the
configured \texttt{hops=3} is an approximation requiring convergence.

Truncated ASD does not simply zero distant blocks of a dense Hessian:
omitted couplings can share seeds with retained entries. For Cartesian mask
$\bm M_k$ and reconstructed $\widetilde{\bm H}_k$ before acoustic treatment,
\begin{align}
  \Delta\bm H_{\mathrm{disc}}&=-(\bm 1-\bm M_k)\odot\bm H,\\
  \Delta\bm H_{\mathrm{cont}}&=\bm M_k\odot(\widetilde{\bm H}_k-\bm H),
\end{align}
\begin{equation}
  \|\widetilde{\bm H}_k-\bm H\|_F^2
  =\|\Delta\bm H_{\mathrm{disc}}\|_F^2
   +\|\Delta\bm H_{\mathrm{cont}}\|_F^2.
\end{equation}
Here $\odot$ is entrywise multiplication. The paper finds contamination
tracking discarded norm down to roundoff; this is empirical, not a general
bound. Acoustic-sum-rule enforcement does not remove either error.

At $0.1\%$ heat-capacity tolerance at 300 K, most paper benchmarks converge
at two hops for MACE, three for PET-S, and four for PET-XS, with slower
zeolite cases \cite{sadMof}. Loaded MOF-5 requires its own comparison
against exact-pattern or feasible dense AD, inspecting signed modes and the
correction across temperatures. Convergence can be nonmonotonic near mode
thresholds. Periodic image folding also requires cell/supercell convergence.

\subsection{Normal modes and quantum harmonic heat capacity}
\label{sec:normal-modes}

Symmetrize the Hessian, enforce the acoustic sum rule, and mass weight:
\begin{equation}
  \sum_j H_{i\alpha,j\beta}=0,
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  D_{i\alpha,j\beta}
  =\frac{H_{i\alpha,j\beta}}{\sqrt{m_i m_j}},
  \qquad
  \bm D\bm e_k=\omega_k^2\bm e_k.
\end{equation}
Uniform translations should have zero restoring force. Unlike the upstream
SADMOF observable, which maps negative eigenvalues to zero, the local
calculation preserves signed imaginary frequencies. Strict hybrid assembly
rejects unexpected imaginary modes and allows at most three translational
near-zero modes. Exploratory outputs instead discard modes at or below the
threshold (Section~\ref{sec:results-hessians}).

For retained positive modes,
\begin{equation}
  x_k=\frac{\hbar\omega_k}{k_{\mathrm B}T}
      =\frac{h_{\mathrm P}c\tilde\nu_k}{k_{\mathrm B}T},
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  C_{V,k}=k_{\mathrm B}
  \left[\frac{x_k/2}{\sinh(x_k/2)}\right]^2,
  \label{eq:quantum-harmonic-cv}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  c_V(T)=\frac{1}{M_{\mathrm{cell}}}
  \sum_{k\in\mathcal V} C_{V,k},
  \label{eq:harmonic-cv}
\end{equation}
$\tilde\nu_k$ is wavenumber, and $M_{\mathrm{cell}}$ includes guests.
Using cell mass in grams and mode heat capacity in \si{J\per K} gives
\si{J\per g\per K}. Equivalently use $N_{\mathrm A}/M_{\mathrm{molar}}$.
One spectrum supplies the configured 100--\SI{500}{K} curve every
\SI{10}{K}; temperature changes populations, assuming fixed force constants.

\subsection{Optimized minima and the hybrid correction}
\label{sec:hybrid-method}

Relax the equilibrated empty structure directly at fixed cell for a separate
reference. For loaded systems, quench representative independent replicas
with the same MLIP and accept only force-converged, stable minima. Raw thermal
frames are not harmonic references. Current outputs use one geometry per
model/loading from \SI{400}{K}.

For $N_m$ accepted minima,
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  \overline{\Delta c^{\mathrm{har}}}(T)
  ={}&\frac{1}{N_m}\sum_{s=1}^{N_m}
  \Bigl[c_{\mathrm{qn},s}^{\mathrm{har}}(T)
  \\
  &\hspace{4em}-c_{\mathrm{cl},s}^{\mathrm{har}}\Bigr],
  \end{aligned}
  \label{eq:minimum-average}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  s_{\Delta c}^2(T)={}&\frac{1}{N_m-1}
  \sum_{s=1}^{N_m}\Bigl[
    \Delta c_s^{\mathrm{har}}(T)
  \\
  &-\overline{\Delta c^{\mathrm{har}}}(T)\Bigr]^2.
  \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Use the same retained modes for quantum and classical terms, with
$k_{\mathrm B}$ per classical mode. Mixed host--guest motion requires the
complete loaded spectrum.
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  c_P^{\mathrm{approx}}(T)
  &=c_{P,\mathrm{cl}}^{\mathrm{anh}}(T)
    +\overline{\Delta c^{\mathrm{har}}}(T),
  \\
  c_{P,\mathrm{cl}}^{\mathrm{anh}}(T)
  &=\frac{1}{M_{\mathrm{cell}}}\frac{\mathrm d\langle H\rangle_{\mathrm{cl}}}{\mathrm dT}.
  \end{aligned}
  \label{eq:hybrid-cp}
\end{equation}
This combines an anharmonic classical $C_P$ with a harmonic $C_V$ correction,
assuming a small quantum pressure--volume term. Validation below about
\SI{200}{K} remains limited.

Persistent LLPR members must contribute both terms before their spread is taken:
\begin{align}
 \sigma_{\mathrm{model}}^2
  &=\sigma_{\mathrm{cl,model}}^2+\sigma_{\mathrm{har,model}}^2
    +2\operatorname{cov}_i(c_{P,\mathrm{cl}}^{(i)},\Delta c_{\mathrm{har}}^{(i)}),
    \label{eq:paired-model-uncertainty}\\
 u_{\mathrm{hyb}}^2
  &=\sigma_{\mathrm{MD}}^2+\sigma_{\mathrm{har,samp}}^2
    +\sigma_{\mathrm{model}}^2.
    \label{eq:combined-uncertainty}
\end{align}
The covariance is across matched model members. $\sigma_{\mathrm{MD}}$
is the sampling error of the central enthalpy derivative;
$\sigma_{\mathrm{har,samp}}$ comes from independently quenched structures.
Quadrature assumes independence of the sampling and model contributions.

Current single-replica analysis perturbs enthalpy means with Gaussian draws
using autocorrelation-based errors and recomputes the derivative. It supplies
within-trajectory error, not a block bootstrap or between-replica variance.
Bands omit the unestimated harmonic sampling term:
$u_{\mathrm{hyb}}=(\sigma_{\mathrm{MD}}^2+\sigma_{\mathrm{model}}^2)^{1/2}$.
LLPR Hessians use central-model geometries without member-specific relaxation.

\section{Results and discussion}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Available calculations and status}
\label{sec:results-scope}

The campaign contains 66 trajectories: PET-MAD
(\texttt{pet-mad-1.\allowbreak{}5-s-40nn}) and PET-SOL (\texttt{pet-sol-s-best}), three
loadings, eleven temperatures, and one replica per state point. Each
\SI{500}{ps} run contributes 8001 frames after discarding \SI{100}{ps}:
\SI{33}{ns} simulated and \SI{26.4}{ns} analyzed in total.
Hybrid curves span 200--\SI{400}{K}; 175/\SI{425}{K} support centered endpoint
derivatives. Each curve reuses one loaded \SI{400}{K} reference spectrum.

All estimates are \emph{exploratory}: every loaded spectrum has imaginary
modes, and four of six relaxations failed their convergence criterion.
Discarding unstable modes does not satisfy the strict acceptance protocol.
Bands quantify selected sampling and model errors, excluding unstable-mode
bias and unestimated variation across minima.

Trajectory diagnostics come from per-run \texttt{summary.\allowbreak{}json}; central
values and relaxation diagnostics come from
\texttt{heat-capacity.\allowbreak{}exploratory-discard-imaginary} CSV/JSON archives.
Reported bands use refreshed stride-1 evaluation of all 8001 frames with
64 persistent LLPR members, rather than older per-loading uncertainty
columns. Figure sources are recorded in \texttt{results-provenance.\allowbreak{}json}.

\subsection{Thermodynamic sampling and equilibration}
\label{sec:results-md}

Mean temperatures stay within \SI{0.315}{K} of target. PET-MAD/100 at
\SI{300}{K} gives $299.854\pm8.031$ K (mean and standard deviation),
close to the equipartition width
$T\sqrt{2/(3N-3)}=\SI{8.058}{K}$. This checks kinetic sampling, not
configurational ergodicity. Pressure fluctuations span
$0.80$--$1.51\times10^3$ bar with mean errors of 9--18 bar;
PET-MAD/100 gives $4.65\pm13.11$ bar, compatible with the \SI{1}{bar}
target. Physical enthalpy always uses imposed pressure.

\subsection{Cell response, framework motion, and methane mobility}
\label{sec:results-structure}

Both models predict contraction at 50/100 guests and expansion at 150 over
175--\SI{425}{K} (Figure~\ref{fig:results-md-overview}). PET-MAD volumes
change from 17146.8 to 17019.9, 17170.2 to 17108.4, and 17210.4 to
\SI{17274.4}{\angstrom\cubed}, respectively. Across models the changes
are about $-0.75\%$, $-0.37\%$, and $+0.3$--$0.4\%$.
Densities at \SI{300}{K} are about 0.677, 0.752, and
\SI{0.825}{g\per\centi\meter\cubed}, largely reflecting added guest mass.
Because $C_P-C_V\propto\alpha_V^2$, contraction does not imply a negative
heat-capacity difference; compressibility is also needed to quantify it.

peak near \SI{4}{\angstrom}. Mobility and crowding support the need for
anharmonic MD; a single harmonic geometry cannot describe diffusion.
Endpoint MSDs alone do not establish diffusion coefficients without a
verified diffusive regime, cell-motion control, and replica statistics.

create a stable minimum. Distinguish insufficient relaxation, unstable
stationary structures, and sparse-curvature errors with convergence checks.
Matching quantum/classical mode masks avoids inconsistent subtraction;
it does not estimate unstable-mode bias. Reusing one \SI{400}{K} geometry
also leaves temperature dependence and minimum-to-minimum variation untested.
LLPR spread at a central geometry does not replace structural sampling.

\subsection{Uncertainty bands}
\label{sec:results-uncertainty}

Figures~\ref{fig:results-error-cea}--\ref{fig:results-error-gaussian}
separate MD sampling, classical LLPR, harmonic LLPR, and paired total
uncertainty. MD errors are about \SI{0.010}{J\per g\per K} at
\SI{300}{K}; model terms dominate. Covariance can make the total smaller
than an individual component (Eq.~\eqref{eq:paired-model-uncertainty}).

\begin{figure}[htbp]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/results-uncertainty-cea.png}
  \caption{Supplied uncertainty decomposition using refreshed CEA member
  curves. Every panel uses all 8001 saved production frames at each
  temperature and 64 LLPR members. The harmonic curve is model spread at
  one fixed geometry, not an uncertainty across minima. The total preserves
  classical--harmonic covariance and excludes an unestimated harmonic
  sampling term. All plotted quantities are standard errors or model standard
  deviations in \si{J\per g\per K}, not variance fractions.}
  \label{fig:results-error-cea}
\end{figure}

For PET-SOL with 150 guests at \SI{300}{K}, classical and harmonic CEA
spreads of 0.108 and \SI{0.155}{J\per g\per K} combine to
\SI{0.103}{J\per g\per K}, with inferred correlation $-0.75$.
PET-SOL/100 instead has correlation $+0.37$ and paired spread
\SI{0.153}{J\per g\per K}. Independent quadrature would miss both effects;
predictive spread is not reduced by $\sqrt{64}$.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/results-uncertainty-gaussian.png}
  \caption{Supplied Gaussian NPT/DPOSE uncertainty decomposition. Central
  hybrid curves, MD sampling errors, harmonic member corrections, and frame
  coverage are identical to the CEA comparison; only the classical member
  spread and its covariance with the harmonic correction change. The smoother
  orange curves result from a different estimator and do not establish more
  accurate uncertainty calibration.}
  \label{fig:results-error-gaussian}
\end{figure}

Gaussian propagation gives larger \SI{300}{K} bands in every case
(Table~\ref{tab:results-cp}), with positive classical--harmonic correlations
of 0.43--0.63. Smoother or narrower bands do not demonstrate calibration.
Recorded $\operatorname{var}(\beta\Delta U_i)$ spans about 2.4 to
$1.42\times10^3$, violating the small-variance CEA criterion; some direct
reweighting effective counts approach one. Neither collapsed reweighting
nor untested Gaussian tails validate these bands, whose coverage against
reference electronic-structure data remains unestablished.

The separate \texttt{variance-hybrid} calculation also changes the central
classical estimator. At \SI{300}{K}, hybrid differences from the derivative
route range from $-0.045$ to $+0.064\,\mathrm{J\,g^{-1}\,K^{-1}}$,
with MD errors of 0.022--\SI{0.030}{J\per g\per K} from squared-fluctuation
correlations. Shared trajectories and harmonic corrections make these
cross-checks correlated. Quantitative predictions require stable Hessians,
stationary independent trajectories, multiple quenched structures, and
validated member propagation.

\section{Conclusions}

PET-MAD and PET-SOL give exploratory room-temperature heat capacities rising
from about 0.93 to \SI{1.16}{J\per g\per K} with methane loading. Harmonic
quantization removes 59--63\% of the classical term; guest mobility supports
retaining anharmonic MD. Broad agreement with digitized reference trends
does not establish quantitative accuracy. Stable spectra, independent
trajectory/minimum sampling, and validated paired uncertainty propagation
are needed before interpreting these curves as converged predictions.
